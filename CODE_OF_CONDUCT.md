# Code of conduct

This project builds the NCI Semantic Infrastructure MCP server with the NCI teams who will carry it
forward and the EVS and caDSR teams whose systems it serves. This code states the conduct we
practise, so that everyone who takes part in the repository, its issues, its pull requests and its
reviews can rely on it.

## Our pledge

We make taking part in this project free of harassment and discrimination for everyone, whatever
their age, body, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, experience, nationality,
appearance, race, religion, sexual identity or orientation, or role. We act in ways that keep the
work open, respectful and focused on its purpose.

## How we work

- **Roles, not people.** Code, issues, pull requests and documents name roles (the reviewer, the
  implementer, the project coordinator), never individuals.
- **Findings are about the work.** Review is given in writing on GitHub, says what is wrong and why,
  and points to the evidence. A comment whose first line starts with `## Reviewer` is binding; a
  disagreement with it is raised before acting, in reply to it.
- **One audience.** Everyone who builds on or implements the server is a colleague, given the same
  information and the same courtesy as anyone else on the project.
- **Upstream teams are partners.** A gap in EVS or caDSR is reported to its team with the evidence,
  never worked around or hidden in this server.
- **Say what is true.** Report results as they are, including failures and what was left out.

## Unacceptable conduct

- Harassment, insults, threats or demeaning comments, in public or in private.
- Unwelcome sexual attention, and sexualised language or images.
- Personal attacks, including on someone's competence instead of their work.
- Publishing someone's private information without their consent.
- Repeating conduct that someone has asked you to stop.
- Retaliating against anyone who reports a concern in good faith.

## Reporting

Report a concern to the project coordinator at hannes.niedner@nih.gov. Reports are handled
promptly and confidentially; the person reporting is told what was done.

## Addressing harm

The project coordinator decides on a response in proportion to the harm, and aims to repair it
rather than to punish:

1. **A private note** explaining what was wrong, with a request to stop and, where fitting, to
   apologise.
2. **A warning** with stated consequences for further conduct of the kind.
3. **A temporary ban** from interaction in the repository.
4. **A permanent ban** from the project.

## Scope

This code applies in every project space (the repository, its issues, pull requests, reviews and
discussions) and wherever someone represents the project.
